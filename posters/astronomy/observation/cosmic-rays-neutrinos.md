## Introduction {.abstract}

For four centuries astronomy had one messenger: **light**. But light is absorbed by dust and trapped inside stars. **Multi-messenger astronomy** adds three more. **Cosmic rays** are atomic nuclei from space, **neutrinos** pass through whole planets, and **gravitational waves** are ripples in spacetime itself. Together they see inside events that light alone cannot.

## 1. Four messengers {.wide}

```compare
| Light (photons) | Cosmic rays | Neutrinos | Gravitational waves
What it is | Massless photons, from radio to gamma rays | **90 % protons**, 9 % helium nuclei, 1 % heavier nuclei | Neutral, almost massless particles (under 0.45 eV) in three flavours | A strain in spacetime, h = ΔL / L
Its path | **Straight**, but absorbed by dust and gas | **Scrambled**: bent by magnetic fields, so its source is hidden | **Straight**: through stars and planets | **Straight**: nothing absorbs it
Made by | Stars, gas, accretion disks, the CMB | **Supernova shocks** and black-hole jets | The **Sun's core**, **collapsing stars** (99 % of their energy), jets | **Merging black holes and neutron stars**
Caught by | JWST, Hubble, ALMA, Chandra, Fermi | **Pierre Auger**: 1,660 tanks over 3,000 km² of Argentina | **IceCube**, Super-Kamiokande, KM3NeT | **LIGO**, Virgo, KAGRA, pulsar timing arrays
```

## 2. Cosmic rays: nature's particle accelerator

In 1912 **Victor Hess** flew a balloon to 5,300 m and found that ionising radiation grows **stronger** with height: it comes from space. A cosmic-ray proton hits a nucleus of air about 20 km up, and sets off a shower of a billion particles.

```flow
[Proton from space|navy] -hits air at 20 km-> [Pions and kaons|blue] -π⁰ decay-> [Gamma rays, electrons|amber]
[Pions and kaons|blue] -π± decay-> [Muons reach the ground|green]
```

```logscale unit=" eV"
2 | Visible photon | grey
1e4 | Medical X-ray | blue
6.8e12 | LHC proton | navy
3e15 | The "knee" | amber
5e19 | GZK limit | red
3.2e20 | Oh-My-God particle | red
```

> [!NOTE]
> **Relativity in your living room**: a muon lives 2.2 µs, long enough to travel only 660 m. Muons made 15 km up still reach the ground, about one a second through your hand, because at 0.9994 c their clocks run 29 times slow.

## 3. Neutrinos: catching the ghost

Fusion in the Sun's core makes two **electron neutrinos** for every helium nucleus. Light takes about 100,000 years to work its way out; a neutrino takes 2.3 seconds.

```cards cols=1 style=top
How to catch one | Build a huge target and wait: 50,000 t of pure water at **Super-Kamiokande**, or a billion tonnes of ice 1.5 to 2.5 km under the South Pole at **IceCube**. A rare hit makes a muon or electron faster than light travels in ice (0.75 c), which shines a cone of blue **Cherenkov light** | blue
SN 1987A: neutrinos first | On 23 February 1987, three detectors caught **25 neutrinos** from a supernova in the Large Magellanic Cloud, about **three hours before** its light. A collapsing core sheds 99 % of its energy, 10⁴⁶ J, as neutrinos in ten seconds | teal
```

```equation
title: The Cherenkov angle
\cos \theta = \frac{1}{n \beta}
n | Refractive index: 1.31 for ice, 1.33 for water |
\beta | The particle's speed as a fraction of c |
```

Neutrinos change flavour as they travel, electron to muon to tau and back: the proof that they have mass.

## 4. GW170817: one event, three messengers

Two neutron stars merged 130 million light-years away. Seventy observatories followed.

```timeline vertical
17 Aug 2017, 12:41 UTC | Gravitational waves | LIGO and Virgo record a 100-second chirp | purple
+1.7 s | Gamma rays | Fermi catches a short gamma-ray burst: gravity travels at the speed of light, to one part in 10¹⁵ | amber
+11 hours | Visible light | A kilonova in NGC 4993, forging gold and platinum | red
+9 days | X-rays | Chandra sees the jet | blue
+16 days | Radio | The jet's afterglow | teal
```

On 22 September 2017, IceCube caught one **290 TeV neutrino**. Telescopes turned to its direction and found the blazar **TXS 0506+056** flaring: the first source of high-energy neutrinos.

## 5. Glossary {.wide}

```glossary cols=4

eV, TeV, PeV | Electron-volt, and 10¹² and 10¹⁵ of them
GRB | Gamma-ray burst
GZK limit | Above 5 × 10¹⁹ eV, cosmic rays lose energy on the CMB
Knee | A bend in the cosmic-ray spectrum at about 3 × 10¹⁵ eV
Muon | A heavy cousin of the electron, living 2.2 µs at rest
UHECR | Ultra-high-energy cosmic ray, above 10¹⁸ eV
```

## In short {.summary}

Multi-messenger astronomy gives us four senses. Light images surfaces and gas, cosmic rays bring samples of matter, neutrinos see inside stars, and gravitational waves follow colliding black holes and neutron stars.

### Neutrinos solved a 30-year mystery

Detectors caught only a third of the Sun's neutrinos. Super-Kamiokande and SNO showed why: neutrinos change flavour on the way.

### Cosmic rays date the past

Cosmic rays turn nitrogen into carbon-14, half-life 5,730 years, the clock of radiocarbon dating.

### Gravity keeps pace with light

After 130 million years in transit, GW170817's waves and gamma rays arrived 1.7 seconds apart.
